Bonanno Writing Workshop Free Worksheet

Revising Your Main Claim

Test and strengthen the thesis you already have — so the argument that drives your essay is as clear and sharp as it can be.

1.

Your Current Claim

Write your current thesis or main claim below — exactly as it stands in your draft. Don't clean it up yet.

2.

Test Your Claim

A strong main claim is specific, contestable, and significant. Check each box once you're satisfied, and use the notes to think through how you might strengthen it.

Is it specific?

Is the claim based in evidence? Does it unpack key terms? Does it explain the relationship between ideas? Does it answer questions of "how" and "why?"

Is it contestable?

Is the claim something a knowledgeable reader could reasonably doubt? Or is it merely an observation about something factual or obvious?

Is it significant?

Does the claim answer "so what?" Why does this argument matter? How does it help a reader understand something new or nonobvious about the topic?

3.

Watch Out for Common Pitfalls

Sometimes claims look strong but fall into familiar traps. Check any that apply to your current draft.

Five-Paragraph Essay Claim

Makes interpretive moves but doesn't offer something nonobvious. Its structure suggests one paragraph per example, each proving the same point. Try: choosing a more specific angle, complicating the claim, or defining key terms more precisely.

Overstated Claim

General and absolute — may create a false debate between two options and pick one, ignoring complicating evidence. Watch for "obviously," "only," or "has to." Try: adding specificity and accounting for reader doubt.

Understated Claim

Contains interesting observations, but the connection between them isn't stated — a reader has to guess. Try: spelling out the how/why relationship between your ideas explicitly.

Descriptive Claim

Describes what happens but doesn't make an argument a reader would doubt. Try: asking "so what?" and "yes, and?" about what you've described.

4.

Revise Your Claim

Based on everything above, write a revised version. It may look very different from where you started — that's a good sign.
